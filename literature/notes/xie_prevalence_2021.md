# Xie, Wang & Kong (2021) — Prevalence of Research Misconduct and Questionable Research Practices: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Citekey:** `xie_prevalence_2021` · *Science and Engineering Ethics* 27:41 · doi:10.1007/s11948-021-00314-9 · **PDF:** `files/38845/`

The current best single source for prevalence. Updates and extends Fanelli (2009) — 42 articles, 571 studies, 23,228 participants from 18 countries, published 1992–2020. Where Fanelli restricted to behaviours that distort knowledge, Xie et al. cover *all* forms of RM and QRPs, organized by Steneck's (2007) taxonomy.

## Headline pooled estimates

**Self-reported (admitted by the respondent):**

| Behaviour | k | Pooled | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| RM (≥1 of FFP) | 84 | **2.9%** | 2.1–3.8 |
| Fabrication | 20 | **1.9%** | 1.0–3.5 |
| Falsification | 34 | **3.3%** | 2.2–5.0 |
| Plagiarism | 29 | 3.2% | 1.9–5.5 |
| QRPs (≥1, unspecified) | 229 | **12.5%** | 10.5–14.7 |
| — QRPs in *data management practices* | 72 | **17.6%** | 14.4–21.3 |
| — QRPs in authorship and publication | 95 | 16.0% | 12.3–20.5 |

**Observed (knew of a colleague doing it):**

| Behaviour | Pooled | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| RM (≥1 of FFP) | **15.5%** | 12.4–19.2 |
| Fabrication | 12.4% | 7.5–20.0 |
| Falsification | 10.3% | 7.6–13.8 |
| QRPs (≥1) | **39.7%** | 35.6–44.0 |

Heterogeneity is severe throughout (I² = 94–99%); random-effects models; significant publication-bias indicators on several outcomes. Prevalence varies with response proportion, recall period, career level, discipline (biomedical higher for RM), and country.

## Why this matters for the manuscript

**This is the citation for the asymmetry the manuscript argues is unjustified.** The self-report figures give the cleanest one-line statement of it:

- Fabrication: **1.9%**
- Falsification: **3.3%**
- QRPs in data management: **17.6%**

So the class of behaviour that includes unprincipled exclusion of cases is admitted at roughly **5–9× the rate** of the two categories that our simulations show require comparable effort and produce comparable bias. Same direction as Fanelli (2009), on a larger and more recent evidence base, with the added advantage that Xie et al. break fabrication and falsification apart (Fanelli pools them) — which matters here, because our adding-strategies are fabrication and our removing-strategies are falsification, and their pooled estimates differ by nearly a factor of two despite (per our results) being interchangeable in effort.

Note also the **observed-vs-admitted gap**: 2.9% admit RM, 15.5% have witnessed it. Whatever the true rate, self-report is a floor. This supports the manuscript's framing that mis-calibrated norms (treating exclusion as venial) will bias *admission* rates far more than they bias *behaviour*.

## Suggested use

- **Introduction**, second paragraph, as the primary prevalence citation — preferred over Fanelli (2009) alone because it is more recent, larger, and separates fabrication from falsification. Cite both: Fanelli for the original synthesis and Xie for the update.
- **Discussion / ethics**: the specific numbers (1.9% / 3.3% / 17.6%) are the quantitative version of "treating the first as common and the second as rare." Pair with Geggie (2001), which shows the same asymmetry *within subjects* in a single survey, and with John et al. (2012), which shows it in perceived defensibility.
- If a sentence is needed on why prevalence estimates should be read as lower bounds: the observed-behaviour figures (15.5% / 39.7%) and the meta-regression finding that surveys using the words "falsification"/"fabrication" return *lower* rates.

## Caution

Enormous heterogeneity and evidence of publication bias; these are order-of-magnitude figures, not precise rates. Do not present the fabrication-vs-falsification difference (1.9 vs 3.3) as a tested contrast — the CIs overlap. The defensible claim is the larger one: FFP of any kind sits at a few per cent while data-management QRPs sit near 18%.
